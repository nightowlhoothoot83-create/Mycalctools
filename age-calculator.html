What it shows

Your age in completed years, remaining months and days, total days lived, and the date of your next birthday.

How the age calculation works

The calculator compares your date of birth with the “as of” date you choose. It first counts completed years, then adjusts the remaining months and days so the result reads naturally instead of simply dividing a total number of days by 365. That matters around month ends, leap years and birthdays.

How to use it

  1. Enter the person's date of birth.
  2. Leave the “as of” date set to today for a current age, or choose another date to check age at a past or future point.
  3. Select Calculate Age.
  4. Read the completed years, months and days separately, plus the next-birthday date and countdown.

Useful real-world examples

  • Checking the exact age of a child for school, sport or a birthday invitation.
  • Working out how old someone was on a particular historical date.
  • Seeing how many days remain until a birthday without manually counting calendar days.
  • Comparing two dates when an eligibility rule is based on age at a specific cut-off.

What “years, months and days” means

An age such as 31 years, 4 months and 12 days describes the calendar interval from the birth date to the selected date. It is not the same as saying someone has lived exactly 31.37 years, because calendar months have different lengths and leap years add an extra day.

Important limitations

This is a calendar calculator, not proof of age. For passports, licences, legal eligibility, benefit rules or formal enrolment decisions, use the date shown on the relevant identity document and confirm the rule with the responsible organisation. Time of birth is not used, so the result changes by date rather than by the exact hour someone was born.
