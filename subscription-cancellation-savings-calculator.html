Subscription Cancellation Savings Calculator

See what cancelling, replacing or downgrading a recurring subscription actually saves.

Annual Saving Break-Even

Count the replacement

A cheaper replacement is still a cost. Include it instead of treating every cancellation as a 100% saving.

What this calculator shows

The calculator subtracts any replacement plan and one-off cancellation fee from the recurring cost avoided. It reports the total net saving, the average monthly benefit and how long it takes to recover a switching fee.

How to use it

Use the amount that actually leaves your account, including GST where relevant. Add the cost of a cheaper alternative, choose a realistic comparison period and include any exit, migration or data-transfer charge.

Useful examples

  • Checking whether moving business files to a cheaper cloud plan is worthwhile.
  • Adding up savings from cancelling an unused streaming, app or membership service.
  • Comparing an annual renewal with a monthly alternative before the renewal date.

Important limits

This is a cash-cost comparison. It does not price lost features, staff time, transferred data or productivity. A service that prevents larger costs may still be worth retaining.

A practical cancellation check

Recurring charges are easy to overlook because each payment can appear small. Looking at a full year makes the trade-off clearer. Before cancelling a business service, export essential files, confirm retention rules and check whether an annual contract has an exit date. For household subscriptions, review recent use rather than cancelling something that would immediately be purchased again elsewhere.

Worked example

A $49 monthly service replaced by a $12 plan saves $37 per month. With no exit fee, that is $444 over twelve months. If migration costs $100, the first-year net saving becomes $344 and the switch recovers its cost during the third month.
