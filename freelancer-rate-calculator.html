About the Freelancer Rate Calculator

This calculator turns an annual revenue target and itemised business costs into a day and hourly rate. It does not calculate income tax, super or profit automatically, so include any amount you want reserved for those purposes in the income target or expense fields.

How to Use It

  1. Enter the annual amount the business needs to generate for you before the listed operating costs.
  2. Enter your estimated annual business expenses (software, insurance, equipment, co-working).
  3. Enter realistic billable days for the year after leave, administration, marketing and gaps between projects.
  4. The calculator divides the required revenue by those days and shows an eight-hour equivalent rate.

Key Costs Freelancers Often Forget

Costs differ by occupation. A designer may need software and equipment; a consultant may carry professional indemnity cover; a trades contractor may have vehicles, licences and tools. Add only costs that apply to the business, then test more than one billable-day assumption. The calculated rate is a floor based on the entries, not a promise that the market will accept it.

Most full-time freelancers realistically achieve 25–30 billable hours per week out of a standard 40-hour week. The rest is spent on admin, proposals, invoicing, marketing, professional development, and business management. Planning around 25 billable hours per week is a conservative and realistic starting point.
