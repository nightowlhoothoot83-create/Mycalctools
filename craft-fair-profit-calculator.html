How to calculate craft fair profit

Start with items sold multiplied by the average price. Subtract payment fees, materials and packaging used for the items sold, then the stall fee, travel and other event costs. That leaves cash profit for the event.

To judge whether the fair paid for your time, subtract total hours multiplied by your target hourly pay. A positive cash result and a negative labour-adjusted result mean the event covered its cash costs but missed your pay target.

The break-even formula

Contribution per item = selling price × (1 − fee percentage ÷ 100) − fixed fee per item − materials and packaging. Divide fixed event costs by that contribution and round up to the next whole item. Add your target pay to fixed costs for the labour-adjusted break-even.

If each item has zero or negative contribution, extra sales cannot cover the event costs at those assumptions. Change the price, cost or product mix before relying on a break-even target.

Worked example

Sell 40 items at 20 each: revenue is 800. Materials cost 240, payment fees are 24, and the stall plus travel costs 100. Cash profit is 436. Ten hours valued at 20 per hour cost another 200, leaving 236 after your target pay.

Each item contributes 13.40 toward event costs and time. You need 8 items to cover the 100 in event expenses, or 23 items to cover expenses plus 200 in target pay. This is an illustration, not a sales forecast.

Use a cautious scenario too

Save your expected sales, then lower the items sold by 25–50% and save a second scenario. Compare the possible loss with the cash you can afford to tie up. Include non-refundable bookings, stock purchases and bad-weather risk in your decision.

What this estimate leaves out

The tool costs materials for items sold, rather than treating all unsold stock as an immediate loss. It does not predict foot traffic, buying intent, refunds, sales tax, GST, income tax, insurance or depreciation. Add event-specific charges to other costs, and use actual receipts and professional advice for records and tax obligations. Revenue is not evidence of money received in your bank account.

Why compare cash profit and pay?

A low-fee local market might leave less cash overall but more per hour than a long weekend event. Looking at both measures helps you compare events without hiding the value of preparation and travel time. Keep non-financial goals, such as testing a new product or meeting repeat customers, separate from the profit result.

Frequently asked questions

Does the calculator save my data online?

No. Inputs and saved comparisons are processed in your browser. Saved comparisons use local storage. The page sends no calculator figures to a server. Your browser and the hosting provider may still handle ordinary page access logs.

Is a lower stall fee always better?

No. Compare likely sales, travel, preparation and selling time as well as the booking fee. Use records from your own previous events where available. The calculator cannot verify an organiser's attendance claims.

Can I use different currencies?

The currency selector changes display labels only. It does not convert amounts. Export or clear your saved comparisons before switching currency.
